## Issue 1 (false claim, fixed): the $V_{\tau_0}$ conjecture

After Theorem `thm:price` the paper says: "we conjecture $R_{[Kn+1,T]}(\Cold)\asymp K\beta^2$ when $T-Kn\ge K\beta^2$, which would put $V_{\tau_0}$ at the same order as $R_T(\Cold)$." This is false throughout the theorem's own admissible range, for a reason you can see without simulation: the theorem requires $n \ge 36\lambda L^2/c^2$, and since $c\le 1$ this exceeds the bad-pull cap $8\beta^2+1 = 32\lambda+1$ of Lemma `cat-cap` whenever $L\ge 8$ (indeed whenever $L>1$). So cold LinUCB completes every forced bad pull *inside* the routed window, its tail regret is essentially zero, and $V_{\tau_0}\approx 0$; the entire multiplier is carried by $A_{\tau_0}$. My simulator confirms this exactly: cold tail regret 0.0 at both $K{=}32$ and the certified $K{=}324$, with all bad pulls (mean 81 per direction) inside the window, while $A_{\tau_0} = 30{,}341$ at $K{=}324$. This is not a defect of the theorem — the ratios are untouched — but the sentence must go, and the surrounding narrative should stop implying the template's multiplier flows through $V$. The patch replaces the conjecture with the correct statement: which of $A_\tau$ and $V_\tau$ carries the saving is an accounting matter that depends on whether cold's counterfactual exploration falls before or after $\tau$; the mechanism (coverage bought at $\varepsilon/c^2$ instead of $1/L^2$) is identical, and on real streams where cold exploration outlasts any affordable $\tau$ (your AmazonCat figure) the same mechanism shows up as $V_\tau$. That framing is also more honest about what the AmazonCat paragraph in Section `sec:amazon` can claim.

## Issue 2 (math error, fixed): Theorem `thm:handoff` truncation factor

The elliptical-potential lemma bounds $\sum\min\{1,\|x\|^2_{V_t^{-1}}\}$, and widths can exceed 1 when $L^2>\lambda$, so the stated bound needs a factor $\max\{1, L/\sqrt\lambda\}$ on the determinant term. The proof waves this away with "absorb into $\beta_T$," but $\beta_T$ is pinned down explicitly two sections earlier, so the statement as written is wrong whenever $\lambda < L^2$ — which includes your own experiments ($\lambda=0.1$, unit-norm features, so the factor is $\sqrt{10}\approx 3.16$). Patched in both the statement and the proof. This is precisely the class of "stated rate does not match the proved rate" slip that reviewer 6dJQ punished last round; do not leave another instance of it.

## Issue 3 (diagnostics claim falsified, fixed): "positive $\widehat N$ is necessary"

Appendix `app:diagnostics` claims a positive net value $\widehat N(\tau)$ is *necessary* for a multiplier. I evaluated the rule on the template, where ground truth is known. At $K{=}32$ the handoff multiplies by 3.2× in simulation, yet the warm widths on the bad arms stay above the gap, so $\widehat\Phi$ does not move at all and $\widehat N(\tau_0) = -\varepsilon Kn < 0$. Even at the certified $K{=}324$ (multiplier 3.4×), $\widehat\Phi$ falls only from 1.000 to 0.941 and $\widehat N$ is negative for any tail shorter than about 210k rounds and a sub-1% fraction beyond that — nowhere near the "one third" threshold. The reason is structural: $\widehat N$ prices only the *future*-rounds channel ($V_\tau$), while the template's gain flows through the *concurrent* channel ($A_\tau$), and $\widehat\Phi$'s clipping at the gap hides savings that keep widths above the gap but still eliminate hundreds of forced pulls. The patch downgrades the claim (negative $\widehat N$ argues against a tail-channel multiplier only) and adds the estimator that covers both channels: a *dry run* — simulate cold LinUCB and warm-started LinUCB pre-runtime on held-out contexts with label rewards, no LLM calls, and compare $\widehat R_{T'}(\Cold)$ against $\widehat C_{\Exp}(\tau')+\widehat R_{\text{tail}}(\text{warm})$. `pre_runtime_estimates.py` implements everything: the $\widehat\Phi$ columns your Table `diagnostics` TODO asks for, $\widehat C_{\Exp}$, $\widehat N$, and the dry run, from a single `.npz` of held-out features, labels, and LLM proposals (all of which you already have from the principal-angle computation). Note the dry run is also the honest answer to why small/base/large Flan-T5 differ at equal accuracy: if the dry-run multiplier separates large from small/base, the "equal accuracy, unequal information" story is confirmed; the current identical 85.1° angles cannot confirm it (see Issue 5).

## Novelty assessment

Relative to the two papers in your project: Chen et al. is the empirical phenomenon plus a CORRAL-style algorithm, no theory of this kind; the ICLR submission ("LLM-Derived Priors and Adaptive Calibration") proves an additive exploration-phase bound under a strong LLM-accuracy assumption ($|s_t(a)-\mu_t(a)|\le\Delta_\alpha$) — its Theorem 1 says the hybrid is no worse than the better of two bounds, which cannot express, let alone certify, a multiplier, and its clipped-UCB mechanism never asks *which directions* the LLM's actions cover. Your paper's identity + instance-catalog approach answers a different and sharper question (when can the combination *beat both*, and by how much), and the two flagship results — the offline/online separation pair on one instance, and the price-ratio multiplier with the confidence radius cancelling — are, as far as I can tell, new. The decomposition itself is elementary and the paper wisely says so; the honest inversion of your own earlier $\Psi$ claim into a theorem ("the determinant term cannot certify a multiplier") is a real contribution because it corrects an error that the concurrent literature (including the ICLR paper's framing of warm-start value through covariance shrinkage) is likely to keep making. Positioning risks are the price-vs-allocation-lower-bound lineage (now cited and differentiated) and the fact that $\Phi_t$ is a standard intermediate quantity (the paper already concedes this, which is the right move). Net: the theory is now defensible in a way the old paper was not; acceptance will turn on whether the experiments deliver the Predicted column and the promised baselines, because the abstract's final sentence ("the one configuration with a large estimated net value is the one that multiplies") is currently an unverified claim about numbers that do not yet exist — and per Issue 3, make sure that sentence ends up describing the dry-run estimate, which can actually support it, rather than $\widehat N$, which on your own template cannot.
